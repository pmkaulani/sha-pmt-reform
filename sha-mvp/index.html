Informal Economy Measurement Challenge

The Challenge of Assessing Non-Salaried Households

Under Regulation 18(1) & (2) of the Social Health Insurance Regulations, 2024, non-salaried households contribute 2.75% of income determined through means-testing, subject to a minimum of KSh 300 per month. Means-testing is conducted pursuant to Regulation 21(6) using the formula in the Fifth Schedule. In the informal sector, assessing irregular income presents distinct economic and methodological questions.

Asset Illiquidity vs. Cash Flow

A household may inhabit an inherited dwelling or small parcel of family land yet experience volatile, near-zero seasonal cash flow. Estimating annual income primarily from physical housing characteristics risks assigning contributions disconnected from monthly liquid resources.

Gross Turnover vs. Net Disposable Surplus

Informal micro-retailers handle cash turnover that reflects wholesale restocking, transport, and commercial stall fees. Treating gross receipts as personal income without deducting direct operating costs overstates disposable household capacity.

Non-Earned and Fiduciary Flows

Loans, family remittances, and custodial group funds (chamas) carry distinct economic meanings. Debt creates repayment obligations, and group savings belong to others. They should not be treated as regular earned income.

Traceable Calculation Records

Without an itemised calculation trace, citizens and caseworkers cannot inspect how an assessed contribution was derived, which deductions were applied, or what information should be corrected during an administrative review.

Informal Livelihoods
Over 80% of Kenya's working population operates in non-salaried, informal, and agricultural activities with irregular cash flow.
Statutory Rates
Regulation 18(1) sets 2.75% of income with a KSh 300/mo minimum under Regulation 18(2), while Regulation 19 governs financial assistance.
Data Protection
DPA 2019 Section 35 and Regulation 22 require meaningful human intervention and explainability in automated decision-making.
Research Methodology

The Structured Income Ledger & Evaluation Framework

This research prototype explores a structured household income ledger that itemises livelihood sources, deducts allowable operating expenses, and produces an arithmetic calculation trace. All models operate as research hypotheses for external examination.

1. Multi-Source Income Categorisation

Distinguishes earned livelihoods (agriculture, micro-retail, casual labour) from non-earned flows (loans, remittances, chama funds), preventing non-income receipts from inflating the contribution base.

2. Itemised Operating Expense Deductions

Permits recording direct business costs (stock purchases, raw materials, commercial rent, transport) to compute an indicative net surplus rather than assessing gross turnover.

3. Observation Window Controls

Requires multi-month records and enforces full 12-month coverage for seasonal activities to prevent extrapolating short harvest peaks into unrealistic annual earnings.

4. Transparent Calculation Breakdown

Produces a deterministic, step-by-step arithmetic waterfall from declared earnings to final contribution, enabling caseworkers and citizens to review every factor.

Institutional Engagement

A Proposal for Collaborative Technical Dialogue

This project is an independent research prototype developed to examine whether structured income recording, itemised expense deductions, and transparent calculation breakdowns can support SHA's assessment operations.

We do not seek immediate operational deployment.

We are proposing an evidence-based technical dialogue with the Social Health Authority and relevant policy stakeholders to review the income-recording methodology, inspect evaluation safeguards, and determine what empirical microdata would be necessary for independent validation.

All software, documentation, and evaluation protocols are maintained for transparent academic and institutional review. For technical dialogue inquiries: pmkaulani@gmail.com.
